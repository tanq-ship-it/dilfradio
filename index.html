                The Lineup

                Here's a sample of artists that are currently in the soundtrack of my life. I know I am always on the hunt for new music and I am sure you are too. Take a look and a listen. I hope you find something that makes you smile. - Kevin

                FREQ // 01

                Marcus King Band

                A South Carolina blues-rock outfit led by guitarist Marcus King, who started gigging alongside his bluesman father as a kid and built the band's sound into a fusion of soul, blues, rock, and jazz. His playing and singing have carried him from the jam-band circuit to a Grammy nomination, with a catalog that keeps stretching into gospel, folk, and cosmic Americana.

                FREQ // 02

                Tab Benoit

                A Louisiana guitarist and singer out of Houma whose bayou blues runs thick with Cajun seasoning and not much else, no pedals, just a Telecaster and a Category 5 amp. He's also spent decades fighting for the Gulf Coast wetlands through the organization he founded, Voice of the Wetlands, so the swamp shows up in both his sound and his second job.

                ▶ embed playlist link here
                FREQ // 03

                Gary Clark Jr.

                An Austin guitarist and singer who fuses blues, rock, and soul with touches of hip hop, mentored early on by Austin blues legends and often described as carrying the future of Texas blues on his shoulders. Multiple Grammy wins later, he's still rooted in that same gritty, soulful tone.

                Sights and Sounds

                The backstory behind music, artists and the times they live in fascinate me. No album, no song and no artistic career is made in a vacuum. Here's some stories and insights of my own that may pique your interest.

                Anders Osborne: the show I missed, the new appreciation I found

                I first caught Osborne live at Jazz Fest about five years ago and walked away floored, the kind of set that rearranges what you think a guitarist can do. I saw him twice live after that, sold each time on him as a live force, but I'd never really sat with the studio records. I was ready for my third visit with him at Garcia's in Chicago. Life got in the way and I had to cancel. So I've spent the week digging into his catalog instead, and it's turned into its own kind of find, the songwriting, the tone, the patience in his playing on record, none of which I'd given proper attention until now. Here's a little taste of what he does.
